## Mission and History

The Society of the Cincinnati in the State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations was founded in 1783 as part of the broader establishment of the Society of the Cincinnati, an organization created by officers of the Continental Army and their French counterparts at the close of the American Revolutionary War. Its purpose was to preserve the ideals and fellowship forged during the struggle for independence, as well as to promote the principles of liberty and responsible citizenship in the new republic. Membership was originally limited to officers who had served honorably, with eligibility passing hereditarily to their eldest male descendants, ensuring continuity of the society’s legacy.

In Rhode Island, the society has maintained a relatively small but historically significant presence, reflecting the state’s modest number of eligible Revolutionary War officers. Over time, it has focused on historical preservation, education, and commemorating the contributions of its members to the founding of the United States. The Rhode Island branch participates in the general society’s activities while also supporting local initiatives, including the preservation of documents, artifacts, and sites connected to the Revolution. Most recently relating to the Battle of Rhode Island and the statue of the Marquis Rochambeau in Newport. The Society continues to serve as a link between past and present, upholding the memory of those who helped secure American independence. The archives are held at the Redwood Athenaeum in Newport.
